Vault no. 0001 · client-side keys

Our servers hold your vault as ciphertext only.

Your master password is stretched with Argon2id, using 64 MiB of memory per guess, on your device before anything is encrypted or uploaded. We never receive the key, so we cannot be made to hand it over.

What the server can read, field by field

Encryption uses XChaCha20-Poly1305 with a per-item nonce. Site names are encrypted too, so a breach of our database does not reveal which banks you use.

Stored per vault item
FieldServer storesServer cannot see
Site and usernameStoredInside the encrypted blobThe domain, the login name

Master passwordNot storedOnly a salt and a login proofThe password, and the key derived from it
MetadataStoredItem count, last-modified time, sync device IDsNothing here is hidden; we list it so you can judge it

If you forget the master password, we cannot reset it

At setup you get a recovery key, printed once. It decrypts a second copy of your vault key, so you can set a new master password without us.

Lose the master password and the recovery key, and the vault is gone. That is the cost of us not holding a key.

Print it. Do not photograph it.

What Vaultix stops, and what it does not

A password manager is one layer. These are the cases we have designed for, and the one we cannot fix.

  • Our database is stolen

    ProtectedThe attacker gets ciphertext. Each guess at your master password costs 64 MiB and about a second of compute, so a long passphrase holds.

  • A fake login page asks for your master password

    Partly protectedAutofill matches the exact domain, so it stays silent on a lookalike. Typing the password by hand still defeats this.

  • Malware on your computer

    Not protectedIf something can read memory or the keyboard while the vault is unlocked, it sees what you see. Keep the OS patched.
